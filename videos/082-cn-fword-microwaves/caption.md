- Ovens run at 2.45 GHz, an unlicensed ISM band; it is NOT a resonance of water (that's ~20 GHz). Water molecules are polar (bent,

- At 2.45 GHz the waves reach roughly 1–3 cm into food; deeper parts heat by conduction from the outer layer (the frozen middle);
  ice absorbs far less than liquid water. Same sources.
- The metal cavity reflects the waves into a standing-wave pattern of hot and cold spots (~6 cm apart, half the 12.2 cm wavelength);
  the turntable moves food through it. Same sources.
- Sharp metal edges (a fork's tines) concentrate the field and can arc. Standard microwave-safety guidance.
- Percy Spencer, a Raytheon radar-magnetron engineer, noticed a candy bar melt in his pocket (~1945; the bar's kind is disputed, the
