- DOMS timing + cause: soreness begins ~12–24 h after unaccustomed exercise, peaks 24–72 h, subsides by ~5–7 days; it follows
  eccentric (lengthening-under-load) work and is attributed to microscopic damage to muscle fibres + the inflammation after it
  (Cheung, Hume & Maxwell 2003, "Delayed onset muscle soreness: treatment strategies and performance factors", Sports Med 33(2);
  Harvard T.H. Chan DOMS sheet https://www.hsph.harvard.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/143/2016/10/9_2015Delayed-Onset-Muscle-Soreness-doms.pdf ;
  ACSM's "Delayed Onset Muscle Soreness" brochure, via https://www.active.com/fitness/Articles/What-is-Delayed-Onset-Muscle-Soreness ).
  Isometric work causes much less soreness and concentric work little to none.
- Not lactic acid: blood lactate returns to pre-exercise levels within about an hour, well before DOMS begins
  (Examine.com https://examine.com/outcomes/muscle-soreness/faq/does-lactic-acid-build-up-cause-muscle-soreness ; Scientific American
  https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/why-does-lactic-acid-buil/ — lactate right after exercise correlates poorly with soreness days later;
  concentric work also makes lactate but no DOMS).

- Repeated bout effect: one bout of eccentric exercise protects against damage from the next; after a single session + 2 weeks' rest the
